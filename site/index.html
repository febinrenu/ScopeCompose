02 — The correction at the centre of the project

One question gives the wrong answer.
Two questions give the right one.

An earlier revision of this design asked only “do these scopes overlap?” and treated overlap as conflict. That cannot tell a passage restating the general rule for a specific group from a passage carving out a real exception — both have nested scope. The fix is to decide applicability and outcome agreement independently, then combine them. Neither determination alone yields the right answer for any row.

03 — What is already solved, and what is not

The gap is between documents.

Three literatures touch this problem and none closes it. The honest version of the claim is narrower than the one this project started with, and the narrowing came from actually reading the closest work rather than from a reviewer.

  • Span-Grounded Deontic Trees (Chen et al.) already names Silent Scope Omission and handles nested exception structure — recursively, and well. Credit where it is due: the phenomenon is not ours to claim.
  • ConditionalQA supplies conditional answers but assumes the conditions live in one supplied document.
  • Defeasible NLI models a rule being overridden, as a sentence-pair judgement, with no retrieval and no scope algebra.
The narrowed claim

Conditional conflict resolution is addressed within a single document. It is not addressed when the rule and its exception are retrieved from separate documents, where no shared structure links them and the resolver must decide whether a scope relation exists at all.

This is the Structure–Grounding Gap. SG-DT grounds its tree in one document's span structure; cross-document retrieval destroys exactly that structure. 37% of the verification set confirmed the narrowed form. The wider claim — that the phenomenon is unaddressed — is not defensible and is not made.

Three findings that already stand.

71 permutations over 128 records, 65 of 71 non-positional. Branch roles derive from the applicability relation, never retrieval order.

The mechanism, not just the number. A gov.uk guide is one CMS document with many parts, each with its own URL. Ten of seventeen batch-1 pairings were one document wearing two URLs. More importantly, organisations state their carve-outs beside the rule, in the same document — which is precisely why the cross-document case the literature leaves open is also the hardest one to collect. That is a finding about the world, and it is the reason the benchmark needs a constructed tier.

2 · The annotation gate, and what it cost

Axisκnpe

Four-way scope relation1.000140.541

Pilot 9 only. Never the pooled figure — an earlier pilot reported κ on 34 instances where only 18 were informative, which reads as a stronger result and is a weaker one. The [1.000, 1.000] bootstrap interval is an artifact, not a confidence statement: every resample of a sample with no disagreement is also perfect, so the interval collapses regardless of n. The code now prints that caveat beside the number.

Reaching a defensible κ took nine pilots, and each one failed in a way worth writing down. One batch was voided entirely because the build step emitted instances with no query text and gave annotators no way to supply one — κ came back at −0.013 and a second defect was hiding it, because the agreement tool pooled every batch together.

Both are now impossible: the CLI refuses to label a query-less instance, agreement is scoped by batch prefix, and a pass whose timing is implausible for genuine reading is rejected by pass_plausibility before κ is computed at all. One pass was discarded on that basis and the batch re-run.

07 — The part most projects leave out

What we cannot claim yet.

The instrument is built and tested. The measurement has not been taken. The experiment designated as able to falsify the central claim has run once, at twelve instances, and it favours the baseline. Reporting that as prominently as a positive was committed to in code before the number existed.

Not measurable at the routing step. The pipeline arm scores a branch as survived if its pair routed to compose, so the code can only ever record preserved or suppressed — distorted and hallucinated are never assigned, and SCR divides an empty denominator. The baseline's 0.333 distortion comes from a real generated answer put through the aligner. The two are not comparable, so they are not compared.

Denominator, stated up front: the four headline metrics score gold branches, and only 42 of 128 instances carry branch structure (83 branches). The remaining 86 have detection labels and no branch annotation yet. Hatched figures above mean cannot support a conclusion — not bad result.

This run read data that is now held out. The twelve instances were picked by file order, before splits existed. Under the assignment now in the corpus they straddle all three: 5 test, 4 train, 3 dev. So the one headline experiment corresponds to no defined split and is majority test data. It is recorded here rather than quietly re-run, and benchmark/data/decisive.json now carries the instance ids, the seed, the commit and all 24 per-branch outcomes so anyone can check. The re-run at 137+ instances has to be on a defined split.

And those 42 are not mined prose. All of them are authored wp1_* probe instances — 42 of 42 are construction=split, separation=synthetic_split. Zero mined instances carry branch annotation. Every branch-scored number on this page, including the ones below, is therefore measured on constructed splits of single documents rather than on the cross-document case the project exists to address. That is the most load-bearing limitation here, and the reason the annotation backlog — not more engineering — is the critical path.

Counterintuitive, and worth resisting the intuition: a lower discordant rate needs fewer branches. The effect being sized is a difference over all branches, so a low discordant rate means it is concentrated in a small subset — easier to see, not harder. Two systems that mostly agree but differ sharply where they differ are cheaper to distinguish than two that disagree everywhere by a little. A unit test asserting the intuitive direction failed; the model was right.

Can claim today

  • A formal four-way scope relation with an executable routing table, and a benchmark schema that enforces it.
  • Order invariance as a structural property: 1.0000 over 71 permutations, 65 of them assigning a role to something other than the first-listed passage.
  • Tier-1 scarcity with its mechanism — a result about where cross-document conditional conflicts live.
  • A metric suite (PR / SR / HCR / SCR) with provenance recorded on every judged number. The LLM judge path is unvalidated and unreachable by default.

Cannot claim yet

  • That composition beats selection. One run, n = 12, interval spanning zero, pointing the other way.
  • Anything about redundant or opposed at scale — two instances each.
  • Any judged metric: the LLM judge is implemented but not yet validated against human labels at the 0.89 bar.
  • Prevalence of conditional conflict in real retrieval. The corpus cannot support it and the code refuses to compute it.
