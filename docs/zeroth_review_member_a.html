My module · where it sits

I decide whether two retrieved passages should be selected between, or composed

Everything upstream of the resolution operator: retrieve, detect, classify, and determine the formal relation between competing claims.

Passage A — general rule International transactions incur a 3% fee.
Passage B — valid exception Fees are waived for premium-tier cardholders.

Both are true. They describe different scopes. A selection-based system ranks the sources and emits the winner, deleting the other branch along with the condition that governs it — and answer-correctness cannot see that happen.

05

A2 + A3 · detection and classification

The detector architecture is settled by experiment, not by assumption

Three variants, compared head-to-head on one cell of the confusion matrix — because that cell, not aggregate accuracy, decides whether the pipeline ever reaches the case it exists for.

A2 — two-stage, cost-aware

K = 5 gives C(5,2) = 10 pairs per query. Sending all of them to an API is what makes a full-corpus run infeasible on a capped tier.

  • Stage 1, local. DeBERTa-v3 NLI cross-encoder plus hand-authored cue features and a learned head over embedding interactions.
  • Stage 2, selective. Only pairs inside a tuned uncertainty band escalate to an API call.

The escalation band is tuned on a dev split and reported as a cost/accuracy curve — the escalation rate is the API cost of detection.

A3 — three architectures

  • Lexical + NLI features. Inspectable; its decisions read off a coefficient table.
  • Fine-tuned transformer. DeBERTa-v3-base end-to-end, no hand-authored features.
  • Structured entailment. The cross-encoder called twice — condition- and outcome-entailment scored separately — then combined by the A4 rule.
The headline is one cell. Distractors dominate the label distribution, so accuracy can look healthy while every real exception is routed to selection. Both directions of the factual/conditional cell are reported, and a variant that collapses onto one label is excluded as degenerate rather than ranked.
Selecting on one direction alone is gameable: a detector that labels everything conditional scores a perfect conditional-leak while being useless. Found by running the comparison, and fixed.
06

A4 · the hard part

“Is this even a conflict?” needs two determinations, not one

Applicability and outcome agreement are decided independently and then combined. Deciding only applicability cannot tell a restatement from a real exception.

Refinement One scope strictly inside the other, outcomes differ there. A genuine exception. → compose
Disjoint Scopes do not intersect. Outcome comparison is vacuous. → compose trivially
Redundant Scopes overlap or nest but outcomes agree throughout. A restatement. → merge, do not compose
Opposed Scopes overlap, neither contains the other, outcomes disagree. → fall back to selection

Why both determinations

A passage restating the general rule for a specific group has nested scope, exactly like a real exception. Only the outcome separates them. Calling it a refinement makes the resolver compose a branch that does not exist — inflating preservation while corrupting the answer.

Exact where possible

Where a scope decomposes into typed attributes — an ordered tier, a numeric range, a category set — subset and disjointness are set operations with definite answers, not prose judgements handed to a model. Undecidable cases fall through to entailment explicitly, and the split is reported.

82% typed on representative passages
The two hardest annotation boundaries — refinement vs. redundant, and refinement vs. opposed — get their own confusion-matrix cells rather than being folded into four-way accuracy.

Results so far · what is measured, and what is not

Two properties already hold; the rest awaits the annotated corpus

Both results below are structural — they follow from the design rather than from how realistic the test data is, which is why they can be shown now.

Order invariance 1.0000 Every scope relation, branch role and confidence identical under all tested passage permutations. 66 of 72 role assignments went against positional order — so the check is not passing vacuously.
Cost of dropping the class 95.0% Exception branches lost at the routing step when the conditional class is removed and pairs are relabelled factual, as a four-class detector would emit.
NLI-filter branch loss 26.8% A reasonable-looking baseline that drops the “inconsistent” passage — which, on a rule/exception pair, is the exception.

Why order invariance matters: if the answer changed when passages were reshuffled, every downstream preservation number would be a function of retrieval order rather than of the method. Branch roles derive solely from the applicability relation — whichever branch has the wider scope is the default, full stop.

Honesty guardrail These are not benchmark results. They are measured on hand-built and generated instances, because the annotated corpus does not exist yet. Two further numbers the tooling produces — the escalation-band curve and exception recall — are currently fixture artifacts and the tools say so in their own output: the curve saturates on templated data, and 65% of generated passages are near-duplicates, which floors retrieval recall for reasons unrelated to the retriever. Nothing here is quoted as a benchmark number.
Owned metrics: binary detection F1 · five-class accuracy and per-type F1 · the factual/conditional confusion cell · four-way scope-relation accuracy · order-invariance pass rate. Owned baselines: standard RAG, rerank-top-1, NLI-filter.

Risks I own

  • Detector novelty. The inspectable design is deliberately modest — resolved empirically by comparing all three variants rather than assuming the modest one wins.
  • Annotation boundary. Refinement vs. opposed is expected to be the hardest judgement. Explicit decision procedure, κ reported for that axis specifically, third-reviewer adjudication.
  • Tier-1 scarcity. Genuinely multi-source rule/exception pairs may be rare. Mitigated by a named Tier 1 / Tier 2 split and a yield trial before committing to the corpus target.
The falsifying experiment A frontier long-context model given all passages and asked for the same structured branch output, scored on the same metrics. If it matches our preservation rate, the central architectural claim does not hold — and we report that as prominently as a positive result.
Expected outcome stated as a hypothesis, not a number: on conditional instances, selection-based methods post acceptable answer-correctness alongside high suppression and low preservation, while composition preserves those branches at comparable correctness.
